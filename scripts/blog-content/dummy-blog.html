Endometriosis is a chronic condition that may affect the pelvic health, menstruation, fertility, and general quality of life of a woman. Endometriosis develops when tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus, such as in the ovaries, fallopian tubes, and pelvic tissues. Symptoms can vary greatly from one patient to another, so diagnosing and treating this condition is essential.

What Is Endometriosis?

Endometriosis is a condition where the lining of the uterus, or endometrial tissue, develops outside the uterus. Such tissue may react to hormones during the menstrual cycle, resulting in inflammation, pain, and adhesion of tissues.

Endometriosis may occur in the ovaries, fallopian tubes, pelvic lining, bowels, bladder, or any other close regions. The severity of the symptoms may not be proportional to the severity of endometriosis in a particular woman. Thus, a woman may have serious endometriosis with mild symptoms, and vice versa.

Common Symptoms of Endometriosis

It will be helpful for you if you are able to identify the symptoms. They include:

  • Severe or increasing menstrual cramps
  • Pelvic pain
  • Pain during and/or after sex
  • Pain while urinating or having a bowel movement, especially during menstruation
  • Heavy or irregular menstrual bleeding
  • Bloating, tiredness, or digestive problems
  • Pain in lower back or abdomen
  • Difficulty in conception

Menstrual pain is not supposed to be taken as a natural symptom of menstruation. When your symptoms affect your work or make you feel uncomfortable about performing your usual activities or planning your fertility, it would be wise to visit an endometriosis doctor.

How Is Endometriosis Diagnosed?

Your visit will start with a thorough interview regarding your symptoms and medical history. You may be asked questions about the severity of period pains, your menstrual cycle, previous therapies, problems during sexual intercourse, bowel and bladder symptoms, and your reproductive plans.

According to your complaints, further investigation could include:

Ultrasound of pelvis: This test can reveal the presence of endometriomas of ovaries and other pelvic abnormalities.

MRI: Sometimes, MRI provides additional information regarding deep endometriosis and structures involved.

Laparoscopy: Minimally invasive surgery can be offered in certain cases. It can give an opportunity for a specialist to investigate pelvic organs and, if necessary, treat endometriosis.

Endometriosis Treatment in Delhi

There is no single treatment that works for every woman with endometriosis. The treatment approach depends on factors such as age, symptoms, disease severity, previous treatment, and whether you are planning a pregnancy.

1. Medication

Doctors may recommend pain-relieving medicines to manage symptoms. Hormonal treatments may also be used to reduce or suppress the hormonal stimulation associated with endometriosis.

The choice of medication should be personalised and discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

2. Laparoscopic Surgery

For selected patients, laparoscopic surgery may be recommended to remove endometriotic lesions, endometriomas, or adhesions. The objective is to relieve symptoms while preserving healthy reproductive tissue whenever possible.

Surgery is not necessarily required for every woman with endometriosis, so an individual assessment is essential.

3. Fertility Treatment

Endometriosis can sometimes affect fertility by contributing to inflammation, adhesions, ovarian involvement, or changes in pelvic anatomy. However, many women with endometriosis can conceive naturally or with appropriate fertility treatment.

Depending on individual circumstances, treatment may include fertility planning, ovulation-related treatment, intrauterine insemination (IUI), or Surgical Treatment in India such as laparoscopic and hysteroscopic surgeries.

If endometriosis is affecting your ability to conceive, discussing both endometriosis management and fertility goals with your doctor can help create a more comprehensive treatment plan.

Endometriosis and IVF: What Should You Know?

For women with endometriosis who are experiencing infertility, IVF may be considered depending on factors such as age, ovarian reserve, fallopian tube status, sperm parameters, previous treatments, and the severity of endometriosis.

However, IVF is not automatically required for every woman with endometriosis. A fertility specialist can assess your individual situation and recommend whether trying naturally, undergoing surgery, considering IUI, or proceeding with IVF may be appropriate.
