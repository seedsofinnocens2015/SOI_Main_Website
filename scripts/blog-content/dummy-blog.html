Ovarian cysts are among the commonest disorders of gynaecological health experienced by women of various ages. An ovarian cyst is a relatively common occurrence in women’s reproductive life and occurs especially in the reproductive years of the woman. Although most of these cysts are harmless and resolve themselves, some may need professional attention.

Understanding what an ovarian cyst is, its symptoms, causes, and treatment methods will be helpful to women in seeking medical help when necessary.

What Are Ovarian Cysts?

An ovarian cyst is a small sac filled with liquid that develops on or in the ovary. There are many different reasons why a woman can have cysts on her ovaries. This may occur due to normal processes that occur in the ovary because of the menstrual cycle.

Most of these cysts do not have any noticeable symptoms. Some cysts may cause pelvic pain and bloating among other symptoms.

What Causes Ovarian Cysts?

Perhaps one of the most frequent questions women would want answers for is, what causes ovarian cysts?

The causes depend on the type of cyst. Some ovarian cysts usually form normally as part of the menstrual process and are called functional cysts, which are considered harmless.

Some of the other causes or conditions that may be responsible include:

  • Hormonal changes
  • Changes during ovulation
  • Endometriosis
  • Changes due to pregnancy
  • Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
  • Abnormal growth of cells in the ovary

Pelvic or Reproductive Disorders

Not all ovarian cysts are caused by a serious underlying disorder. However, proper evaluation is essential for the cyst, which is persistent, large, and complex, or symptomatic.

Types of Ovarian Cysts

There are various types of ovarian cysts, and identification of the type is essential for doctors to determine the treatment options, including observation, medicines, or surgery.

Functional Cysts

Functional cysts are among the most common types of ovarian cysts. The cysts are caused by the normal process of the menstrual cycle.

Follicular and corpus luteum cysts are two common types of functional cysts.

Dermoid Cysts

Dermoid cysts, also referred to as mature cystic teratomas, consist of different kinds of tissues. These cysts can appear during the reproductive period and may need to be removed surgically, depending on their size, symptoms, and possible complications.

Endometriomas

Endometriomas are cysts related to endometriosis. Endometriomas may form due to endometrial-like tissue affecting the ovaries.

Cystadenomas

Cystadenomas originate from epithelial cells of the ovarian surface. Cystadenomas can be very large and need more investigation or even surgery.

Complex Ovarian Cysts

Some ovarian cysts are considered complex because they contain something other than liquid. The complex ovarian cyst needs more testing or evaluation by the doctor.

What Are the Symptoms of Ovarian Cysts?

Most ovarian cysts don't cause any symptoms. But, if there are symptoms, they might be as follows:

  • Pelvic pain
  • Discomfort in the pelvis
  • Pain on one side of the lower abdomen
  • Pressure sensation in pelvis
  • Abdominal bloating
  • Pain during specific activities
  • Painful sexual intercourse
  • Menstrual pattern changes
  • Frequent urge to urinate if the large cyst presses the bladder
  • Painful and sudden pelvic or abdominal pain may need urgent care, especially in conjunction with nausea, vomiting, syncope, or dizziness.

Are Ovarian Cysts Dangerous?

One of the common questions is: are ovarian cysts dangerous?

Most ovarian cysts are benign, and their danger lies in possible complications. Functional cysts usually go away by themselves without intervention.

However, there are some types of cysts that should be looked at by a doctor. If a cyst is very large, it may cause rupture or twisting of the ovary, a condition called ovarian torsion.

It is estimated if there is a risk of any dangerous condition according to the patient's age, symptoms, type and appearance of the cyst, etc.

That is why follow-up and evaluation are crucial if a cyst does not go away or is suspected to be dangerous for any reason.

What Size of Ovarian Cyst Is Dangerous?

The other frequent question is: what size of ovarian cyst is dangerous? There is no particular size of an ovarian cyst that is dangerous. Treatment of a cyst is not determined by its size alone.

A small cyst with suspicious symptoms should be evaluated, but a big simple cyst is not necessarily treated.

Doctors typically take into account:

  • The size of the cyst
  • Whether it is simple or complex
  • How it appears in an ultrasound
  • Whether it is getting bigger
  • The patient’s symptoms
  • The patient’s age and whether she is postmenopausal
  • Any blood tests or further imaging when clinically indicated

Consequently, rather than just taking the size into account, it is essential to examine the ovarian cyst as a whole.

Interpreting a Malignant Ovarian Cyst Ultrasound

Assessment of a malignant ovarian cyst involves identifying ultrasound imaging characteristics that could potentially indicate malignancy and necessitate further testing.

Yet, the ultrasound alone cannot give a definitive diagnosis. Doctors consider multiple factors, such as ultrasound findings, medical history, age, and any other tests, if needed.

Factors that could trigger additional examination include:

  • Solid areas inside the cyst
  • Thick cyst wall or septations
  • Irregularity
  • Alterations in blood flow
  • Fluid in the abdomen in certain clinical settings

It should be noted that the presence of a complex ovarian cyst or an abnormal ultrasound does not necessarily mean that the cyst is malignant.

Treatments for Ovarian Cysts

There are several different ways for ovarian cysts treatments, depending on the type, size, and characteristics of the cyst.

Observation

Many simple and functional cysts may be observed through repeated ultrasounds. This would work well in cases where the cyst is relatively small, benign, and not producing any symptoms.

Medication

If a cyst produces certain symptoms, such as pain or irregular menstruation, medication may be prescribed. It will depend on what is causing the cyst.

Surgery

In some cases, surgery may be required in case of an ovarian cyst that is:

  • Large or persistent
  • Painful
  • Complex
  • Growing in size
  • Complicated

Requiring Removal for Diagnosis or Treatment Purposes

In cases where possible, minimally invasive surgery may be performed. It all depends on the characteristics of the cyst and the requirements of the particular patient.

Women who are worried about their fertility should consider it while making decisions about treatment options.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Can ovarian cysts go away by themselves?

Yes. Some ovarian cysts can go away by themselves and don't require any surgical intervention. Your doctor may recommend doing imaging of the cyst periodically.

2. Are ovarian cysts dangerous?

No, most ovarian cysts aren't dangerous. Yet some bigger or more complex ones may require additional medical investigations.

3. What can cause ovarian cysts?

Ovarian cysts can appear because of normal ovulation, hormone changes, endometriosis, pregnancy-associated changes, and other ovarian conditions.

4. What is the dangerous size of an ovarian cyst?

There is no specific size considered dangerous. The size is always evaluated together with the appearance of the cyst and the symptoms, growth, and other individual parameters.

5. When do I need to have surgery because of ovarian cysts?

When the cyst is big, complicated, or associated with complications and concerns.
