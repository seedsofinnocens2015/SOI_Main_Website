What Is an i-Pill?

An i-pill is an emergency contraceptive drug that contains the drug levonorgestrel. An emergency contraceptive works by preventing or postponing ovulation. They are not abortive drugs, meaning that they do not cause abortion but only prevent pregnancies.

Emergency pills like the i pill should only be taken in cases of emergencies. They should be taken in case of unprotected sexual intercourse or failure of contraception due to factors like slippage or breakage of a condom.

The effectiveness of emergency contraceptives increases with time; however, depending on which emergency contraception is used, there are varying times when each emergency contraceptive is supposed to be taken.

What Are the Main ipill Uses?

The main purpose of using emergency contraception is to reduce the chances of getting pregnant through unprotected sexual intercourse.

Some of the common ipill uses include cases like:

  • Unprotected sexual intercourse
  • Breakage or slippage of a condom
  • Failure of another method of contraception or improper usage

The emergency contraception was developed as an emergency contraception. The emergency contraception does not prevent sexually transmitted diseases and therefore should not be used as a replacement for the use of condoms when protection against STIs is necessary.

Another key thing to note is that the emergency contraception is not a protection from the future unprotected sexual intercourse. In case you engage in unprotected sexual intercourse after using the emergency contraception, consult a healthcare provider about your choices.

How Does an I-Pill Tablet Work?

Levonorgestrel is the active substance in the ipill tablet. Levonorgestrel works mostly through the delay or the inhibition of ovulation. Ovulation is the process of release of an egg by an ovary. Ovulation can be inhibited or postponed and consequently decrease chances for fertilisation.

Therefore, the effectiveness of emergency contraception depends on what stage of the menstrual cycle a person is at. The emergency contraceptive pills do not interfere with the already established pregnancy.

Since the ipill tablet is hormonal emergency contraceptive, the menstrual cycle might be affected temporarily.

Side Effects of the I-pill

Most females tend to handle levonorgestrel emergency contraception effectively, with side effects being minor and short-lived. As per the WHO, possible side effects could be nausea, vomiting, tiredness, and mild irregular vaginal bleeding. Possible reactions include:

1. Nausea

Nausea or sickness can be one of the possible side effects of emergency contraception. Nausea normally tends to be temporary and resolves on its own.

However, if you face vomiting that is persistent or severe, you should see a doctor or a pharmacist. Vomiting shortly after taking emergency contraception could mean that you have not absorbed the drug properly.

2. Abdominal pain or cramps

A few women might experience some mild abdominal pain or cramps similar to period cramps.

While mild pain might be temporary in nature, severe or unusual lower abdominal pain need not necessarily be side effect of emergency contraception. Pain needs to be assessed medically if the pain is severe, persists or is associated with pregnancy.

3. Headaches

Headaches could be experienced following hormonal changes from emergency contraception.

However, if the headache is unusually severe or persistent, then medical advice should be sought.

4. Fatigue

Another side effect that you might experience after using ipill is tiredness or reduced energy levels. Enough rest and proper nutrition may help until your body becomes used to the regular schedule again.

5. Light bleeding

One of the most notable side effects of ipill is alteration in the menstrual cycle.

Some women can experience light bleeding or spotting before the beginning of the menstrual cycle.

6. Changes in the next period

The next period can be earlier or later than usual. Moreover, there might be some changes in the intensity of blood flow during the next period.

These are just some of the common i-pill side effects.

I-Pill Side Effects: How Long Will They Last?

For most women, side effects that result from i pill will clear up after a few days.

It depends on individual cases since menstrual cycles differ from one woman to another. In some cases, women have noted side effects only in connection with their period instead of right after consuming the pill.

In cases where the side effects persist for long, are severe, or cause a lot of discomfort, it's best to contact a doctor instead of thinking that they are i pill side effects.

Can an I-Pill Affect Your Period?

Yes, Taking i pill can affect when you'll have your next period.

The changes in your period may be characterized by:

  • Your period coming earlier than normal
  • Your period coming later than normal
  • A light period
  • A heavy period
  • Spotting before your period
  • Changes in your menstrual cycle

These side effects tend to be short-lived. In case you have a delayed period, it may be wise to take a pregnancy test. If you feel abnormal pains with your possible pregnancy, seek medical help as soon as possible.

Will Emergency Contraceptive Pills (i-pills) Lead To Infertility?

The main question which comes up while discussing pregnancy with females is whether there will be any impact on the fertility of a woman due to emergency contraceptive pills.

As of now, there is no proof or evidence available regarding the effects of such emergency contraceptive pills leading to infertility or delaying the fertility in women. As per WHO, the use of emergency contraceptive pills does not lead to any effect on future fertility.

Hence, the use of an i pill cannot be assumed to be one of the reasons for failure to conceive. If you are planning to conceive but facing troubles doing so, then there could be many other causes such as ovulation issues, age-related problems, fallopian tube issues, endometriosis, male fertility and others.

Does i-Pill Cause Infertility?

There is no scientific evidence suggesting that the levonorgestrel emergency contraceptive pills cause infertility in women. Fertile status can normally resume in women after using emergency contraception. Nevertheless, emergency contraception should not be used as an alternative contraception method but only in emergencies. In case you need emergency contraception frequently, it is recommended that you visit a gynecologist and discuss other contraception methods.

What Are Ways to Avoid Side Effects of I-Pill?

If you want to know how to avoid i pill side effects, then the best thing to do is take care of your comfort needs.

Here are some general ways you can follow to avoid side effects of ipill:

  • Drink water
  • If you have nausea or vomiting, it is important that you drink sufficient amounts of water.
  • Eat small meals
  • When feeling sick, it might be easier for your stomach to cope with small and light meals.
  • Ensure enough sleep
  • Because fatigue may result from using emergency contraception, having sufficient rest time may prove helpful.

Monitor your periods

Record the days you took your emergency contraception and when you got your next menstruation. It will be easier to detect changes and provide valuable data to the doctor, if necessary.

Do not take additional drugs

Do not increase doses or take any other medicines due to your fears about side effects. Consult a specialist if you have doubts about taking another dose, especially after vomiting.

If you are looking for tips on how to lower the i pill side effects, then know that you do not need to resort to various home-made methods or hormonal medications to "clean" the pill from your body.

When to See a Doctor?

While the i pill side effects usually subside on their own, certain symptoms might require a visit to the doctor. Consult with a healthcare provider if you have:

  • Intense or continuous abdominal pain
  • Heavy and unusual vaginal bleeding
  • Continuous vomiting
  • Significant delay of the menstrual period
  • Positive result of a pregnancy test

Symptoms you find abnormal

An unexpected and unusual abdominal pain in combination with suspected pregnancy requires an immediate visit to a doctor because an ectopic pregnancy is rare but potentially dangerous. The same advice is provided by the NHS regarding significant delay and unusual pain during the next period.

What If You Feel Vomiting After an ipill Tablet?

The vomiting that appears a few minutes after taking the emergency contraception may have an effect on the amount of medicine you get into your system.

According to WHO recommendations, if vomiting occurred within the first two hours after taking the emergency contraceptive pill, you need to retake the pill. However, it is better to contact a pharmacist or a doctor about the appropriate course of actions if you are unsure about the time.

Are Emergency Contraception and Regular Contraceptives One and the Same?

Not at all, While emergency and regular contraceptives have some similarities, their applications are different.

Emergency contraception is meant for use in circumstances when there is an urgent need to prevent pregnancy from unprotected sexual intercourse or contraceptive failures.

Regular contraception is used for pregnancy prevention. Methods include condoms, contraceptive pills, IUDs, implants and others.

If you often find yourself in need for emergency contraceptives, consultation with a gynecologist to find the appropriate regular contraceptive would be more beneficial and could also solve your worries regarding recurrent i pill side effects.
