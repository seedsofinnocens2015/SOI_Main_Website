What is a Bicornuate Uterus?

A bicornuate uterus, commonly referred to as a heart-shaped uterus, occurs when the upper part of the uterus, namely the uterine horns, forms as a two-horned structure despite having a single united uterine cavity. Think of it as an imaginary division of the uterus into two equal parts that creates an indentation or depression at its top end. This is not a condition that appears suddenly; rather, it is a congenital Mullerian anomaly, meaning a person is born with it. The development of such a uterine anomaly takes place in the prenatal stage because of the partial development of the ducts of the uterus.

What are the signs of a heart-shaped uterus?

A heart-shaped uterus does not present with symptoms at all, especially before pregnancy occurs. This is because of the absence of symptoms; women who have a heart-shaped uterus would never come across such a condition until they undergo ultrasonography. But still, there are certain symptoms for women having a bicornuate uterus, such as:

    Does a heart-shaped uterus influence your fertility?

    According to most medical professionals, while some other problems with the uterus may be linked to fertility issues, the heart-shaped uterus will not normally have any negative effect on your ability to get pregnant or have the fetus implanted in the lining of the uterus. In other words, in terms of conception, you will face no problems, yet complications arise during pregnancy.

    Pregnancy and a heart-shaped uterus

    The ability to get pregnant will not be influenced by the heart-shaped uterus. Nevertheless, it poses some risks for a good pregnancy due to increased miscarriage rates. It is suggested that this is caused by decreased space within the uterus. Besides, abnormal contractions may occur.

    Risk of miscarriage

    It is unfortunate, but the risk of miscarriage cannot be determined easily in every individual case. As mentioned by a study by the UK’s Royal College of Obstetricians & Gynaecologists, the risk lies somewhere between 1.8% and 37.6%. The wide gap is caused by the fact that there are not many studies on the subject, and all of the few existing ones have involved women with one, two, or three miscarriages. They do not make any statistical distinction between early miscarriage and late miscarriage. One observation made from the study, however, is that the latter seems to be more prevalent among women with a heart-shaped uterus. That could also be caused by their weak cervix.

    Cervical insufficiency

    Weakness in the cervix is another symptom commonly observed among women with a heart-shaped uterus. The cervix is the lowest part of the uterus that opens into the vagina, and in healthy women, it remains closed throughout pregnancy until it begins to soften and open up as preparation for delivery. However, in the case of cervical insufficiency, the cervix becomes soft before it should, resulting in miscarriage or premature birth.

    Greater probability of Cesarean Section

    In addition to the above risks, a heart-shaped uterus increases the probability that your baby might be in the breech position. In such a situation, the baby is in an inverted position; i.e., his/her head is at the top, with the bottom directed downward toward the cervix during delivery. As such, doctors watching your pregnancy may opt to suggest a cesarean section.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    1. Is a bicornuate uterus genetic?

    A bicornuate uterus is a congenital anomaly that is present at birth and is generally not considered a strongly hereditary or genetic condition that is passed down in families.

    2. Can a bicornuate uterus cause infertility?

    Yes, it can sometimes lead to infertility, preterm birth, or recurrent miscarriages.

    3. Do all bicornuate uterus need surgery?

    No, Surgery is only required if there are repeated pregnancy losses or both cavities are small.

    4. Is a bicornuate uterus dangerous?

    It is not dangerous for general health, but can affect reproductive outcomes.

    5. How is a bicornuate uterus different from a septate uterus?

    A bicornuate uterus has two cavities with a divided fundus, while a septate uterus has one fundus with a septum inside.
