What is an AMH Test?

An amh test measures the level of anti-müllerian hormone (amh) in a woman's blood. Amh is produced by ovarian follicles and indicates the number of eggs (or ovarian reserve) available to a woman. Knowing amh test meaning helps women who want to get pregnant. It determines the likelihood of getting pregnant and helps your doctor determine what is the best treatment plan for you.

AMH Test Meaning and Importance

The amh test meaning refers to the ability of a woman to provide future children with high-quality reproductive cells. While an amh test does not measure egg quality, it gives an indication of how many eggs are available in a woman's ovaries. It is common for doctors to order the amh blood test for women who are experiencing difficulty conceiving or who are planning to postpone having children. The test results are very useful for making diagnosis of how many amh has been produced based on the test results and make decisions regarding fertility.

AMH Test: When to Do?

The amh test is a question you should ask yourself often, and when should I get an amh test? An AMH test can be performed on any day of your menstrual cycle, so you don’t need to worry about timing the AMH test with other hormonal testing, which is a nice benefit to using the AMH test. Planning a family, having irregular periods and/or receiving fertility treatment are situations where the AMH blood test may be useful because it provides you with information on your reproductive health.

AMH Test Normal Range

Understanding a person's fertility status is aided by familiarity with the AMH test's normal range; typically, high AMH levels are indicative of polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS), normal AMH levels represent an adequate ovarian reserve, and low AMH levels suggest a decreased ovarian reserve. It is important that the interpretation of a person's AMH test normal ranges be made only by a qualified medical provider and may differ from person-to-person with regard to age and/or medical history.
