Are IVF and Test Tube Baby the Same?

Indeed, in common language usage, the two concepts are very close to each other. Nevertheless, in a medical context, the first is the actual procedure while the latter is a colloquial name used to refer to a baby conceived via IVF.

As part of the IVF process, a fertilization of an egg by sperm cells is performed in vitro, that is, in a controlled lab environment. Following fertilization, if a viable embryo is formed, it is transferred to the womb of a woman with the possibility of subsequent pregnancy.

The baby is not grown in a test tube. The name “test tube baby” refers to the fact that the initial fertilization process takes place in vitro.

What Is IVF Treatment?

IVF treatment is an example of assisted reproductive technology (ART) that is aimed at helping individuals or couples conceive when natural conception would be hard.

Typical steps in IVF include:

1. Ovarian Stimulation

Some fertility medications could be given to make ovaries produce more mature eggs in one cycle.

2. Egg Retrieval

When eggs become mature, they are retrieved from ovaries through a medical procedure called egg retrieval.

3. Sperm Retrieval

A sperm sample is obtained from the male partner or a donor.

4. Fertilization in the Laboratory

The eggs and sperm cells are combined in order to achieve fertilization. Sometimes, a procedure called ICSI, which involves direct injection of a sperm cell into an egg, is recommended.

5. Embryo Growth

The fertilized eggs continue developing in a laboratory under supervision.

6. Embryo Transfer

One or several embryos can be transferred into the uterus. Successful implantation may result in pregnancy.

That is why IVF is called the “test tube baby” method.

Why is IVF referred to as Test Tube Baby Treatment?

The term "test tube baby" was used widely because fertilization is one of the main processes in IVF and it takes place not in the human body but in the laboratory.

It is necessary to remember that a baby is not born as a result of this process in the test tube.

An embryo is incubated in the laboratory for some time under special conditions, and then after the embryo transfer, further development takes place in the womb as usual.

These days, doctors refer to this method as IVF because it is more correct to do so.

Who might need IVF treatment?

There are various reasons why one may need to consider IVF treatment. Based on the individual's situation or the couple's condition, some of the fertility cases that might be treated by IVF include:

  • Cases of blocked or damaged fallopian tubes
  • Male factor infertility problems
  • Cases of endometriosis
  • Fertility problems involving ovulation
  • Unexplained infertility problems
  • Age-related infertility problems
  • Past unsuccessful fertility treatments
  • Donor eggs or sperm
  • Fertility preservation or genetic cases.

Nevertheless, it's not necessarily the case that IVF is the best option for all people. There are many other factors that one needs to consider before settling for IVF treatment. Some of the factors to consider include age, ovarian reserve, sperm quality, medical background and past fertility treatments.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is IVF and test tube baby same?

IVF is the process itself, and "test tube baby" is an informal name of a baby who has been conceived via IVF. Although the concepts are very close, they still describe something different.

2. Why is IVF considered to be a test tube baby?

The reason why the method is called like that is the location of fertilization, which occurs in vitro. Nevertheless, the baby does not develop in the test tube. Embryo transfer means that the pregnancy develops in the woman’s uterus.

3. Can a test tube baby be born in a regular way?

Yes, A test tube baby, whose embryo has been transferred successfully and there was a conception, can be born either via vaginal or Caesarean delivery.

4. Is IVF the only method for having a test tube baby?

"Test tube baby" is not a procedure by itself. It is usually associated with IVF.

5. Is IVF therapy applicable to all individuals?

Not necessarily, It may depend on several aspects including age, fertility diagnosis, ovarian reserve, sperm quality, previous treatment, etc. A fertility expert will advise you whether you are a candidate for IVF therapy.
