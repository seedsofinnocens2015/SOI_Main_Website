Anterior vs Posterior Placenta: Understanding Placental Position During Pregnancy

Pregnancy brings along several milestones for mothers, and one of the most common findings from the 18-22 week anomaly scan is the position of the placenta. Many parents-to-be have been known to ask about the meaning behind the anterior placenta, posterior placenta, and placenta posterior, wondering if these conditions are harmful to the health and delivery of their babies. Luckily, both anterior placenta vs posterior placenta are types of normal placentas. Although the position of your placenta may determine when you can start feeling your baby kick, it does not necessarily pose any risks to your pregnancy.

This guide will help you understand Anterior Placenta vs Posterior Placenta, compare them, talk about their symptoms, pregnancy outcomes, and answer some commonly asked questions.

What is Placenta?

Placenta is a temporary organ which grows in the womb during pregnancy and provides vital connection between mother and child via the umbilical cord. This organ transfers oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and removes wastes from the fetus.

Depending on its positioning in the uterus, you may be diagnosed with anterior placenta, posterior placenta, fundal placenta, or lateral placenta.

Anterior Placenta Meaning

The placenta positioned anterior is positioned on the front wall of the uterus.

It is a very common placental position and is generally found in the second trimester ultrasound.

Characteristics of Anterior Placenta

  • Positioned on the front wall of the uterus
  • Baby movements can be felt a bit later
  • First kicks might feel softer because of cushioning effect
  • Ensures a healthy pregnancy
  • Generally, does not affect delivery

    • Posterior Placenta

    Posterior placenta is positioned on the back wall of the uterus near the spine of the mother.

    Also, most ultrasound findings often state the placenta posterior, which is the same meaning as that of posterior placenta.

    What is Posterior Placenta?

    Parents often wonder "what posterior placenta means".

    The answer is quite clear – it simply means that the placenta is positioned on the back wall of the uterus. It is a very normal condition and doesn't mean anything about the health of the baby.

    In the same way, when your ultrasound findings state placenta posterior means, it simply states the position of the placenta and nothing else.

    Anterior vs Posterior Placenta: Key Differences

    Feature - Anterior Placenta - Posterior Placenta

  • Location – Front wall – Back wall
  • Baby movements – Felt slightly later – Felt earlier
  • Cushioning – More – Less
  • Pregnancy outcome – Normal – Normal
  • Delivery – Normal – Normal

    • Both positions support healthy fetal development and are considered completely normal.

    Posterior Placenta Equals Baby Boy or Girl?

    One of the top searches on the internet is does posterior placenta mean boy or girl? The answer to this is no.

    It has not been scientifically proven that posterior placenta is indicative of the baby being a boy. Neither can an anterior placenta tell you if you are carrying a baby girl.

    The best way to find out the gender of your baby is through medical imaging when it is appropriate or genetic testing in some instances. In India, any sort of sex determination before birth is illegal due to the PCPNDT Act.

    What is a Posterior Low Lying Placenta?

    The meaning of a posterior low lying placenta is that your placenta is attached to the posterior part of the uterus but is close to your cervix.

    In many instances, the posterior low lying placenta detected in the second trimester shifts higher due to the growth of the uterus.

    If it stays close to or covers the cervix during your third trimester, you may have placenta previa.

    Does The Location Of The Placenta Influence Fetal Movement?

    The primary difference between Anterior vs Posterior Placenta is the time when the woman feels the fetal movement. In the case of the anterior placenta, the fetus is protected by the placenta, and therefore the movements are softer.

    If the placenta is posterior, the movements are more noticeable due to the absence of intermediate tissues.

    Whichever type of placental location, always see your doctor when you experience low fetal movements.

    Can The Location Of The Placenta Affect Delivery?

    No, Both the anterior placenta and the posterior one do not affect your ability to have a vaginal delivery provided that there are no other complications in pregnancy.

    Among the factors affecting the mode of delivery are the following:

  • Fetus' position
  • Woman's health
  • Placenta previa
  • Delivery process
