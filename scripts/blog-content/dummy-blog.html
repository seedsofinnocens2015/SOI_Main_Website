It's scary when your ultrasound scan result shows bulky uterus or bulky cervix, particularly when you want to conceive. But not all times is the condition linked to a serious problem. For some women, it's a case of a slightly bulky uterus or cervix due to hormones, pregnancy, and age. Others will have the condition due to illnesses such as fibroids, adenomyosis or infections.

It's essential to know what does bulky uterus mean and bulky cervix mean since it can be an issue that will affect your menstrual flow, fertility and your pregnancy. But the good news is that most women will conceive normally even after receiving the proper treatment.

What is a Bulky Uterus?

The bulky uterus meaning implies that there is an enlarged uterus, which is much bigger than what the woman's uterus should be according to her reproductive life stage.

It is worth noting that bulky uterus is not a disease on its own; it is a medical term that points at the presence of another condition. Mild bulky uterus could be considered harmless and just require observation. In case of serious enlargement, the cause of such phenomenon might be uterine fibroids, adenomyosis, hormonal imbalance, and other gynecological problems.

Most of the time, women find out that they have a bulky uterus as a result of routine pelvic ultrasound scan.

What is a Bulky Cervix?

The bulky cervix means that the cervix is enlarged during pelvic exam or ultrasound.

Women usually ask themselves what the bulky cervix means or bulky cervix meaning when they see it in their ultrasound results. Put simply, bulky cervix means that there is an enlargement of the cervix due to some causes.

In case of mild bulkiness, there might be no need for any treatment if there are no symptoms. Yet, a persistent increase in bulkiness requires a consultation of the gynecologist due to possible presence of infection or cervical abnormalities.

Size of Normal Uterus

The size of a normal uterus is:

  • Length: 7-8 cm
  • Width: 4-5 cm
  • Thickness: 2-3 cm

    • An increase in these sizes beyond the stated parameters is considered as a bulky uterus. Ultrasonography is the most accurate way to measure the size of the uterus.

    Causes of Bulky Cervix

    There could be various reasons leading to a bulky cervix.

    Common causes of bulky cervix:

  • Chronic cervicitis
  • Cervical polyps
  • Nabothian cysts
  • Hormonal imbalance
  • Previous vaginal childbirths
  • Cervical infection
  • HPV-caused cervical changes
  • Sometimes cervical tumors

    • Even if the causes of a bulky cervix are harmless, yet, it needs to be investigated to detect the exact cause.

    Common Causes of Bulky Uterus

    The common causes are:

    Uterine Fibroids

    It is a non-cancerous tumor, which usually enlarges the uterus.

    Adenomyosis

    It is a condition in which the lining of the uterus grows within the muscular layer of the uterus.

    Hormonal Imbalance

    High levels of estrogen can lead to the enlargement of the uterus.

    Previous Pregnancies

    Women with previous pregnancy history can have a naturally enlarged uterus.

    Endometrial Hyperplasia

    Enlargement of the lining of the uterus.

    Perimenopause

    Fluctuations of hormones in this phase can cause bulky uterus.

    Symptoms of Bulky Uterus and Bulky Cervix

    The symptoms may be:

  • Heavy Bleeding
  • Painful Periods
  • Pelvic Pain
  • Back Pain
  • Pelvic Pressure
  • Increased Urination
  • Dyspareunia
  • Difficulty Conceiving
  • Irregular Bleeding

    • Vaginal Discharge (in case of bulky cervix)

    Sometimes women with mild bulky cervix or uterus show no symptoms at all.

    How to Diagnose Bulky Uterus and Bulky Cervix?

    It can be recommended to undergo such examinations as:

  • Pelvic examination
  • Transvaginal ultrasound
  • Pelvic MRI
  • Pap smear
  • HPV testing
  • Blood test
  • Hormonal analysis
  • Hysteroscopy (if necessary)

    • Such examinations will be useful in determining the reason for the bulky uterus and bulky cervix.

    Will a Bulky Uterus and Bulky Cervix Influence Fertility?

    Many women wonder whether a bulky uterus is beneficial or harmful for conception. This all depends on the cause of the disease.

    A bulky uterus resulting from fibroids or adenomyosis can impair fertility by:

  • Distorting the uterine cavity
  • Influencing the embryo implantation
  • Blocking the fallopian tubes
  • Increasing the risk of miscarriages

    • Just like a bulky cervix, resulting from chronic infection or cervical abnormalities, can occasionally prevent conception.

    But there is good news - many women successfully conceive despite of the problem.

    Is Bulky Uterus Good or Bad?

    Many individuals have been seen to go online and ask whether a bulky uterus is good or bad. A bulky uterus is not necessarily good or bad. It is just an abnormality that can be diagnosed medically.

    However, it becomes problematic in the presence of:

  • Severe fibroids
  • Adenomyosis
  • Excessive bleeding
  • Pelvic pain
  • Repeated miscarriage
  • Infertility
