Endometriosis: Symptoms, Causes, Treatment and Its Impact on Fertility

Have you ever wondered why your pain from menstrual periods is more excruciating compared to others' pain? Have you experienced continuous pain in the pelvic region, menstrual cramps, or fertility problems? Do not consider it as "regular period pain" anymore! One possible reason is endometriosis.

Endometriosis is one of the most widespread gynecological disorders that negatively impacts a woman's life, reproduction, and psychological state. It is important to know the endometriosis symptoms, its causes, and possible ways of treatment to receive proper medical consultations on time.

What is Endometriosis?

Endometriosis is an illness in which the tissues that are similar to the lining of the uterus (endometrium) start growing outside the uterus. Such tissue can form on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, pelvis, or nearby organs.

It is different from the usual lining of the uterus, as such tissue does not leave the body during monthly periods. Instead, it becomes inflamed, irritated, and forms scar tissue and adhesions.

So, what is endometriosis and what are its causes? It is difficult to say for sure since there are many factors that may lead to the development of endometriosis.

What is endometriosis and causes?

The causes of this disease are still unknown. Yet, scientists suggest that certain factors related to genetic predisposition, hormonal changes, as well as immune system problems could be associated with its development.

Among some possible causes and risk factors, one may find the following:

  • Retrograde menstruation (backward flow of menstrual fluid through fallopian tubes)
  • Family history of endometriosis
  • Certain hormonal factors, including excessive estrogen
  • Immune system problems
  • Reproductive and menstrual factors

The presence of one or more risk factors does not mean that the disease will appear.

What Are the Symptoms of Endometriosis?

Endometriosis symptoms may differ depending on a woman. Thus, some of them experience severe symptoms while others may feel only mild ones or may not have any noticeable signs of the disease.

Among the most common endometriosis symptoms, one may include:

  • Severe menstrual cramps
  • Pelvic pain
  • Pain during or after intercourse
  • Heavy and irregular menstrual bleeding
  • Lower back pain
  • Discomfort during bowel movements, especially during the menstruation period
  • Pain when urinating during the menstruation period
  • Bloating
  • Tiredness
  • Nausea
  • Problems with conception
  • Pain that intensifies during the menstruation period

This is because a search for “20 symptoms of endometriosis” can yield many results, but symptoms themselves cannot determine whether a patient has endometriosis or not. A gynecologist may ask a patient to undergo examinations, ultrasounds, MRIs, or other tests based on their symptoms.

Can Endometriosis Spread to Other Organs?

Another popular question people ask is: Can endometriosis spread to other organs?

While endometriosis mostly affects the uterus, it can affect other organs and tissues, especially those found in the pelvis. In some instances, endometriosis may extend to the ovaries, fallopian tubes, bowel, bladder, and other organs.

However, this does not mean that endometriosis is similar to cancer and spreads through the body in a manner akin to cancer cells. Nevertheless, in extreme or untreated cases, this condition may cause inflammation, adhesions, ovarian endometriomas, and reproductive organ damage.

Is Endometriosis Contagious?

No, endometriosis is not contagious. It is not possible to have endometriosis just because you come in contact with someone with the disease.

This is a chronic gynecological condition that is affected by biological and hormonal factors. If your family members suffer from endometriosis, then the chances of you getting this problem increase, but there is no possibility that you may catch it just by being in contact with an individual suffering from this disease.

How Does Endometriosis Impact Fertility?

For some women, endometriosis may make it difficult to conceive. This could be due to inflammation, adhesions, ovarian endometriomas, or any change in the fallopian tubes.

But this does not necessarily mean that endometriosis will affect your fertility and pregnancy chances. Many women conceive even when they suffer from this disease.

When endometriosis interferes with conception, the fertility specialist checks the ovarian reserve, fallopian tubes, sperm quality, and other reproductive factors before suggesting a treatment plan.

Endometriosis Treatments

A uniform method of treatment is not always applicable to all women. The treatment methods for endometriosis depend on several variables like age, symptoms, seriousness of the condition, fertility concerns, and general health conditions.

Methods of treatment can include:

Medicines

Pain relievers and hormonal therapy might be prescribed by a doctor to control the symptoms of the disease and prevent hormonal stimulation of endometriosis.

Laparoscopic surgery

It is a type of surgical procedure that allows identifying and treating the lesions and adhesions caused by endometriosis in suitable candidates. This procedure could be prescribed in case of severe pain and infertility.

Fertility treatment

If you have trouble getting pregnant, your doctor will prescribe you the fertility treatment according to your specific condition, such as ovulation induction, intrauterine insemination, or in vitro fertilization.
